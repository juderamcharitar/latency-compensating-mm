Dear Editor,

Please consider my manuscript, “How Much Does Limit Order Book Microstructure Add Beyond Quote Geometry? Evidence from Cryptocurrency Limit Order Books,” for publication as a Research Article in the Journal of Futures Markets.

The paper studies a market-microstructure question with direct relevance to electronic trading systems: after conditioning on quote side, distance from mid, and holding horizon, how much incremental predictive information remains in the observable limit order book? Using 269,330 depth-10 Kraken snapshots across BTC/USD, ETH/USD, and SOL/USD, I compare gradient-boosted and recurrent models with an explicit lookup benchmark based only on quote geometry. In the primary forecasting-ordered evaluation, microstructure features improve AUC by 0.0480 on BTC/USD and 0.0596 on ETH/USD, with dependence-aware paired block-bootstrap 95% confidence intervals of [0.0400, 0.0551] and [0.0427, 0.0751]. The corresponding SOL/USD effect is not distinguishable from zero.

Although the empirical setting is cryptocurrency spot markets, the paper’s contribution is a general market-microstructure and trading-system methodology: it isolates the predictive content of book state from information mechanically embedded in order placement and shows how split design, temporal dependence, and validation choices alter conclusions. I believe this fits the Journal of Futures Markets’ interest in market microstructure, liquidity, trading systems, high-frequency and algorithmic strategies, and quantitative methods in electronic markets.
